My first day of class at Ming Chuan University landed somewhere between
excitement and a little bit of shock. New country, new campus, new language
echoing down the hallway, and it was a lot to take in before I'd even sat
down for my first lecture.

## Research Methods: familiar ground

This one felt like home. The material itself wasn't new. I've spent the
last couple of years knee-deep in research back in Vietnam, so terms like
literature review and IMRD summaries are old friends at this point. What
stood out was the professor: genuinely supportive, the kind of teacher who
makes a big class feel like a conversation. Sitting through the first
homework brief actually turned into something more. It gave me a chance to
look back at my own research process and notice how far it's come.

![The Research Methods lecture slide outlining our first homework: find a peer-reviewed empirical study and prepare an IMRD summary](/blog/research-methods-homework.jpg)

_Homework number one: track down a peer-reviewed empirical study, print it,
and write up a short IMRD summary before Monday._

## Intermediate Mandarin: full immersion, no safety net

Then came Mandarin, and the ground shifted. The class was taught entirely
in Chinese, no English safety net, no slowing down for stragglers. It was
disorienting in a way that Research Methods wasn't. Keeping up with spoken
instructions while also trying to formulate answers in a language I'm
still building fluency in was, honestly, a lot.

![The Intermediate Mandarin homework slide with interview-style questions to answer aloud, entirely in Mandarin](/blog/intermediate-mandarin-homework.png)

_The homework I was supposed to have: record full-sentence answers to
questions like "why did you apply to Ming Chuan University" and "do you
think pressure helps you grow," entirely in Mandarin._

But the day had one bright spot saved for last. I won a small in-class
game, and the prize was a pass on that night's homework. Small win, but on
a day this overwhelming, it felt like a big one.

## One day in

Two classes, two completely different feelings. One reassuring, one
humbling. If day one is anything to go by, this chapter in Taiwan is going
to stretch me in ways I didn't fully expect. I'm looking forward to it.
